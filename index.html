Part 2

Descriptive vs. Inferential Statistics

Understanding the two major branches of statistics

📊 Descriptive Statistics

"What does our dataset tell us right now?"


Summarizes and describes the exact data you have measured. Our entire focus today!

🔮 Inferential Statistics

"What can we conclude about a larger population?"


Uses a small sample to make predictions or hypothesis tests about data you haven't seen.

Part 3A

Central Tendency: The Mean (Average)

The sum of all values divided by the number of values

📐 Concept

The standard arithmetic average. Provides a single central balance point for a numerical dataset.

=AVERAGE(O2:O1000001) Excel Formula

🏥 Global Dataset Application

Column: Average Treatment Cost (USD)

$25,010.31

Interpretation: Across our 1,000,000 health records, treatment costs center around $25,010.31.

Part 3B

Central Tendency: The Median (Middle Value)

The exact middle value when data is sorted from smallest to largest

📐 Concept

The 50th percentile mark. Cuts data exactly in half. Not distorted by extreme high or low values!

=MEDIAN(O2:O1000001) Excel Formula

🏥 Global Dataset Application

Column: Average Treatment Cost (USD)

$24,980.00

Interpretation: Exactly 50% of recorded treatment costs are below $24,980.00, and 50% are above.

Part 3C

Central Tendency: The Mode (Most Frequent)

The value that appears most often in a dataset

📐 Concept

Identifies popularity or high frequency. Works for numerical rates as well as categorical text values.

=MODE.SNGL(G2:G1000001) Excel Formula

🏥 Global Dataset Application

Column: Mortality Rate (%)

6.54%

Interpretation: The single most frequently occurring mortality rate recorded in the dataset is 6.54%.

Crucial Concept

Mean vs. Median: The Skewness Story

Why relying on the Mean alone can mislead decision-makers

💡 10-Row Sample Walkthrough

Sample Costs: $21k, $47k, $27k, $144, $8.9k, $42k, $15k, $15k, $7.6k, $9.4k


Mean: $19,693.20

Median: $15,661.50

🎯 The Analyst's Verdict

Two expensive treatments ($47,851 & $42,671) pull the Mean up by over $4,000.


If explaining patient expectations, the Median ($15,661.50) is far more accurate for a typical patient!

Part 4

Spread & Range: MIN, MAX & RANGE

Understanding data boundaries and overall spread

📉 Minimum (MIN)

=MIN(Q:Q)

Output: 50.00% (Recovery Rate)

📈 Maximum (MAX)

=MAX(Q:Q)

Output: 99.00% (Recovery Rate)

↔️ Range (MAX - MIN)

=MAX(Q:Q) - MIN(Q:Q)

Output: 49.00% Total Spread

Reference

Statistics Cheat Sheet

Quick reference guide for Microsoft Excel statistical functions

Statistic What It Tells Us Excel Function When to Use It
Mean Arithmetic average =AVERAGE(range) Symmetrical data without severe outliers
Median Middle 50th percentile =MEDIAN(range) Skewed data or data with extreme values
Mode Most frequent value =MODE.SNGL(range) Popular categories or discrete values
Min / Max Lowest and highest values =MIN() / =MAX() Finding floors, ceilings, and boundaries
Range Total spread (Max − Min) =MAX() - MIN() Measuring data variability & spread
Wrap Up

5 Golden Rules for Data Analysts

Core takeaways to remember in your data career

1. Never Use One Metric

Always examine Mean, Median, Min, and Max together.

2. Beware of Outliers

When data is skewed by extreme values, trust the Median.

3. Speak Plain English

Translate formulas into clear business narratives.

4. Know Your Scope

Descriptive statistics only claims what is in your data.
